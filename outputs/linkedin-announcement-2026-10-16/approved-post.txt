We’re building an open soil spectral library—and inviting builders to help shape it.

Introducing GSIP: the Global Soil Intelligence Project.

Our vision is to connect soil observations from around the world into a shared, traceable reference library for AI research, better soil maps, and deeper understanding of the soil biome.

Smartphones are the accessible field capture tool: guided photographs, a reference card, sample records and environmental context. Instruments provide measured spectra, and laboratories provide verified measurements.

The key is connecting that evidence to the same physical soil sample. Together, these records can support training and evaluating AI models, with clear provenance and uncertainty.

The software is open source. Developers and researchers can fork it, self-host it, build instrument adapters, improve the capture tools, or develop applications on top.

The website and public map are live. Public capture and library uploads are still closed while we complete the required reviews and validation. The map currently displays existing soil-map layers; the global reference library is what we’re building toward.

We welcome developers, soil scientists, spectroscopy researchers and open-data contributors.

# Standard capture procedure for soil photographs

**Status: proposed (WP-COLLECT-6), version `gsip-capture-standard-v1`.** Written from general
photographic practice. It has not been tried with a printed card or a real phone; the
[validation plan](validation-plan.md) is the first test. It opens no collection, changes no
activation gate and replaces nothing in [SPEC §5](SPEC.md#5-capture-protocol-v11): contributing
without a card, without this procedure and without buying anything stays allowed.

## What this is for

Small differences in light, distance, angle, phone hardware and phone software change the
colours a camera records. A photo library is only comparable across phones and days if every
photo is taken the same way and carries a known colour reference. This procedure fixes the way;
the [field kit](field-kit.md) supplies the reference; the acquisition record says what was done.

What it does not do:

- A phone photograph is an **RGB observation**: three broad colour channels. It is not a
  wavelength-resolved spectrum and is never recorded as one.
- Following this procedure yields no soil-property estimate. Nothing here estimates, predicts or
  reports any soil property from a photograph.
- "Calibrated" in GSIP means only that exactly one reference card was detected and read in the
  photo, so a per-photo colour normalisation toward the card's reference values can be fitted
  ([SPEC §17.3](SPEC.md#173-colour-normalisation)). The GSIP cards' reference values are digital
  targets, not instrument measurements of a print, so this is not colorimetric or reflectance
  calibration.

This procedure was prompted by feedback from José Lucas Safanelli (Woodwell Climate Research
Center, Open Soil Spectral Library) in November 2026, who pointed to the standard practice of
drone (UAV) RGB survey photography and to reference colour cards. He has not reviewed this
document, and naming him implies no endorsement.

## The eight rules

These come from how UAV RGB surveys are flown, restated for a handheld phone. The UAV column is
a summary of common practice, not a quotation from one standard.

| #   | Rule                                  | UAV survey practice                                          | Handheld soil photo                                                             |
| --- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Diffuse, even light                   | Fly under steady light; avoid broken cloud and long shadows  | Open shade, overcast sky or a diffuser. Never direct sun, never dappled light   |
| 2   | Camera square to the subject          | Nadir (straight-down) images                                 | Lens axis at 90° to the soil surface: straight down on a tray, square on a face |
| 3   | Fixed distance                        | Constant height above ground, so every pixel covers the same | One distance, set with a physical guide, the same for every photo in a series   |
| 4   | Locked white balance                  | Fixed white balance, never automatic                         | Lock it before the first photo and leave it                                     |
| 5   | Locked exposure                       | Fixed shutter, aperture and ISO for a flight                 | Lock exposure and focus on the soil, not on the card                            |
| 6   | No processing that varies by scene    | No HDR, no scene modes; unedited images                      | HDR off, filters off, portrait and night modes off, flash off, 1× lens, no zoom |
| 7   | A reference target in the same light  | Reference panel photographed with each flight                | One reference card in every photo, flat, in the plane of the soil               |
| 8   | Repeats and a record of what was done | Overlapping images and a flight log                          | At least three photos per sample, and the acquisition record below              |

## Before you start (both procedures)

1. **Permission and safety.** Sample only where you have permission. The
   [sampling rules](SPEC.md#5-capture-protocol-v11) apply unchanged. Do not climb into a deep or
   unsupported pit for a photo.
2. **Clean the lens.** A fingerprint lowers contrast across the whole frame.
3. **Use the main rear camera at 1×.** Do not let the phone switch to the wide or macro lens.
   No digital zoom.
4. **Turn off** HDR, night mode, portrait mode, filters, "scene optimiser" or similar, live or
   motion photos, and the flash. Save as JPEG at the highest quality.
5. **Lock white balance and exposure.** In a camera app with manual ("Pro") controls, choose the
   daylight white-balance preset and a fixed ISO and shutter. In the stock camera app, press and
   hold on the soil until the exposure and focus lock indicator appears. If your phone cannot
   lock white balance, leave that item unticked; the record will say "not stated".
6. **Check the light.** Soil and card must be in the same light, with no shadow edge, no sunlit
   patch and no coloured reflection from clothing, a wall or leaves. Your own shadow counts as
   shade only if it covers the soil and the card completely.
7. **Lay the reference card** flat beside the soil, at the same height as the soil surface, so
   that card and soil are in one plane. Keep fingers, tools and glare off every patch. Use one
   card only: two cards in a photo give no reading.

## (a) Prepared samples

For soil brought indoors or to a table.

1. **Air-dry** the sample at room temperature, out of direct sun, until it no longer darkens a
   paper towel. Do not oven-dry.
2. **Crush gently** to break clods, without grinding stones or grains. Remove roots and litter.
3. **Sieve to 2 mm.** Photograph the fraction that passes.
4. **Fill the tray** from the kit to the rim, at least 1 cm deep, so that no tray bottom shows.
5. **Level the surface** by drawing a straight edge across the rim once. Do not press, polish or
   smear it: a compacted surface reflects differently.
6. **Place the tray** on a matte, neutral, dark or grey background. Put the card beside it,
   raised so that its surface is level with the soil surface.
7. **Light it.** Use open shade outdoors, or indoors one fixed lamp arrangement that never
   changes between samples, with other lights off and no window light mixing in. Record
   "artificial" for a lamp.
8. **Set the distance** with the distance guide: 30 cm from lens to soil surface is the proposed
   default. Hold the phone parallel to the tray (use the camera app's level if it has one), with
   tray and card both wholly in the frame and the tray near the centre.
9. **Take three photos.** Between photos lift the phone away and set it again; turn the tray a
   quarter turn each time. This shows how much the result moves when nothing real has changed.
10. **Check each photo** before moving on: sharp soil, whole card visible, no glare on a patch,
    no pure white or pure black areas on the card.
11. **Record** the procedure ("prepared sample"), the light, the distance and the number of
    photos in the capture checklist.

## (b) Field profiles

For a pit wall, a road cut or a cleaned auger core laid on a board.

1. **Clean the face.** Cut back the face with a knife or trowel from top to bottom to remove
   smeared and dried soil, working to one flat plane. Brush off loose crumbs.
2. **Note the moisture.** Photograph the face in one moisture state and say which (dry, moist
   or wet) in the observation. Do not wet one part only.
3. **Shade the whole face and the card** with a tarp, an umbrella or the diffuser. If the sun
   reaches any part of the face, wait or shade more. Do not photograph a face half in sun.
4. **Fix the card in the plane of the face**: pinned or held flat against it, not leaning on the
   pit floor and not tilted toward the camera. Put the scale beside it, also flat on the face.
5. **Stand square to the face.** The lens axis should meet the face at 90°, level with the
   middle of the part being photographed. Tilting the phone up or down changes both the light
   and the scale across the frame.
6. **Set the distance** with the guide and keep it for the series: 30 cm for a section of the
   face is the proposed default. For a whole deep profile, step back to one fixed distance (for
   example 100 cm) and record it; take the 30 cm sections as well.
7. **Take three photos** of each section, resetting your position between them.
8. **Check each photo** as for prepared samples.
9. **Record** the procedure ("field profile"), the light, the distance and the number of photos.

## What gets recorded

Each observation queued by the app carries an acquisition record
(`gsip-capture-acquisition-v1`). A value the app cannot read, or that you did not state, is
written as `unknown` or `null`; nothing is guessed.

| Field                             | Where it comes from today                                                  |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Device make and model             | Not read by the app. Filled from the photo's own metadata when present     |
| Operating system, app version     | Operating-system family and the app build. No OS version is read           |
| Exposure time, ISO                | Not read by the app. Filled from the photo's own metadata when present     |
| White balance and exposure locked | Your tick in the checklist                                                 |
| HDR off, filters off, flash off   | Your tick in the checklist                                                 |
| Lighting                          | Your choice: shade, overcast, diffuser, direct sun, artificial, not stated |
| Camera angle                      | Your tick ("square to the surface")                                        |
| Distance, width of the scene      | The numbers you enter                                                      |
| Reference card ID and version     | Set only by a card detector, never by a tick                               |
| Calibration status                | `uncalibrated` unless a detector read exactly one identified card          |
| Number of photos of the sample    | The number you enter                                                       |
| Time                              | The capture time of the primary photo                                      |
| Location                          | Nothing, unless you tick the consent box; then one area of about 0.1 km²   |

Three limits to know:

- **The record stays on the phone for now.** The service has no place to store it, and adding
  one needs its own reviewed database change.
- **The app does not detect the card.** Its detector is a stub that always answers "not run", so
  every observation the app queues is `uncalibrated`. A card is read only by the photo checks
  that run after upload ([photo QA notes](../pipeline/PHOTO_QA.md)).
- **The record is private.** It names a device and a time and is never part of a public release.

An uncalibrated capture is never placed in the same export list as a calibrated one. That holds
for capture records written with `pipeline.capture_acquisition.write_partitions`. The nightly
public release still lists each photo's own status inside one file and is not changed here.
